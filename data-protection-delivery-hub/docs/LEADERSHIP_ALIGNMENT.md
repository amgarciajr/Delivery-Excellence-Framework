# Leadership alignment

## Purpose

The Data Protection Delivery Hub is presented as a practice improvement operating system, not as a collection of disconnected module screens. The primary leadership question is: **does this help the practice deliver better, more consistently, with evidence that supports decisions?**

## Role-based expectations

The prototype uses role expectations rather than personal assumptions:

| Audience | What the hub must make visible |
| --- | --- |
| Direct lead | Execution quality, clear ownership, blockers, overdue work, and evidence gaps that need coaching or intervention |
| Practice manager | Repeatable delivery cadence, adoption of standards and reusable assets, recurring failure patterns, and improvement actions |
| Director | Measurable practice impact, portfolio risk, readiness confidence, scale leverage, and decisions or support required |

## Director briefing standard

The Command Center should answer these questions within one review:

1. Is delivery quality and consistency improving?
2. Where is the portfolio exposed or blocked?
3. Can readiness and stage-gate decisions be supported by reviewed evidence?
4. Which reusable standards or lessons are reducing rework?
5. What decision, escalation, or investment is required from leadership?

## Evidence boundary

The named leadership relationships supplied for the demo are context for presentation only. The application does not infer personal expectations from public profiles, store personal profile data, or use real identities in synthetic records. Production authorization and assignment must come from Entra identity, Dataverse security roles, and teams.

## Demo narrative

Lead with the quality outcome, then show the leadership alignment panel, quality metrics, improvement backlog, portfolio health, priority risks, and stage-gate watchlist. Finish with the connection-readiness boundary and the next decisions needed to move from synthetic proof to governed production data.
